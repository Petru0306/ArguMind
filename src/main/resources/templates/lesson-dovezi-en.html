Lesson 4

Evidence pyramid: what convinces the AI referee

Not all “evidence” is equal. A personal story may move people, but a peer-reviewed study changes your “Evidence & Examples” score in ArguMind.

1. Hierarchy (weak to strong)

5 “I have a friend who...” — anecdotes, no generalization
4 Statistics without a source or from popular media
3 Government reports, recognized NGOs, official data
2 Academic studies, meta-analyses, peer-reviewed journals
1 Scientific consensus + explained mechanism + acknowledged limitations

2. One-sentence evidence formula

[Who] + [what they measured] + [result] + [when/where] + [optional limitation]

“OECD (2023) shows countries with mandatory digital education score 18% higher on IT skills at age 15 — but the effect varies by infrastructure.”

3. When you don't have a study handy

  • Use comparative reasoning: “Country X did Y → result Z”.
  • Acknowledge the limitation: “I don't have the exact figure, but the mechanism is...” — honesty beats invention.
  • Don't invent percentages — the AI referee penalizes unfounded claims as logic errors.

4. Mini-test

Which is stronger and why?

A) “Everyone knows pollution is serious.”
B) “The EPA reports CO₂ emissions rose 3% in 2023 vs. 2022 in the energy sector.”

Answer: B — source, metric, time frame.
