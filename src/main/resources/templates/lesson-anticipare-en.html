Lesson 3

Think like your opponent: anticipation in 2 minutes

Good debaters are not “smarter” — they are better prepared. Before you speak, imagine what the other side would say and prepare your response.

1. Map of the 3 likely attacks

For any topic, your opponent will usually use one of these strategies:

  • Premise attack — “your data is outdated / incomplete”
  • Relevance attack — “your example is isolated, not the rule”
  • Cost attack — “your solution is too expensive / dangerous / slow”

ArguMind tip

Write down 3 sentences you would use if you were on the opposing team — then respond to each in 2 sentences.

2. The “Steelman” technique (opposite of strawman)

Strawman — you build a weak opponent to beat easily. Judges and AI notice.

Steelman — you formulate the strongest version of the opposing argument, then show why your position is still superior. That earns respect and logic points.

Example (topic: nuclear energy)

“Even if nuclear is efficient, accident risk and waste storage for generations are serious arguments — and they deserve a response, not silence.”

3. The 2-minute pre-match pack

  1. Write your position in max 12 words.
  2. List 2 arguments + one piece of evidence for each.
  3. Write 1 counter-argument you expect and your short response.
  4. Choose 1 memorable closing line (without exaggerated emotion).
