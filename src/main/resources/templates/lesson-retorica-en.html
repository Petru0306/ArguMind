Lesson 5

Clarity and impact: rhetoric without manipulation

A high “Clarity & Rhetoric” score does not mean being poetic — it means being easy to follow, memorable, and fair. Emotion is seasoning, not the main course.

1. Rule of 3 ideas

The audience's brain retains about 3 points. Winning structure:

Opening — position in 1 sentence + why the topic matters
Core — 2 arguments (not 7), each with evidence
Closing — restate position + answer the strongest objection

2. Hooks that are not manipulation

Good — real question: “What happens to democracy when news is filtered by algorithms?”
Good — contrast: “We want free speech — but we accept automated moderation every day.”
Risky — fear without data: “Our children will live in a digital dictatorship.”

3. Short sentences, precise words

Instead of Try
“very many problems”“three main risks”
“people generally believe”“survey X shows 62%...”
“it is clear that”“from premise A follows B”

4. A closing that sticks

The last sentence introduces no new ideas — it summarizes and offers a clear choice:

“If we accept that [shared premise], then [your position] is the only one that solves [problem] without [major cost]. That is what I propose — and what I defend.”
